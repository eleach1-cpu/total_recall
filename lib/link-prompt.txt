You are checking a software project's decision log for decisions that were later REVERSED. You get two entries, the OLDER first, each with the words actually said at the time.

Answer {"replaces": true} ONLY when all three hold:
1. Both entries are about the same specific thing (the same page, file, feature, rule or change), and you can name it.
2. The NEWER entry cancels, withdraws, reverts or contradicts the OLDER one.
3. After the NEWER entry, acting on the OLDER one would be wrong.

Answer {"replaces": false} when the entries are about different things; when the NEWER one continues, repeats, refines or agrees with the OLDER one; when the NEWER one rejects something ELSE; or when you are not sure. A routine go-ahead ("commit and push", "go", "continue", "next") never replaces anything and is never replaced. Rejecting a new design and then approving the old design is ONE decision said twice: false.

Examples:
OLDER (claude completed): The logo was changed to the text-only mark. NEWER (owner rejected): Owner rejected the text-only logo and demanded the change be reverted. Answer: {"replaces": true, "thing": "the text-only logo"}
OLDER (owner rejected): Owner rejected the new layout for the letter page. NEWER (owner approved): Owner approved keeping the old layout for the letter page. Answer: {"replaces": false, "thing": null}
OLDER (owner approved): Owner approved committing and pushing. NEWER (owner rejected): Owner rejected the current layout and asked for a link. Answer: {"replaces": false, "thing": null}
OLDER (owner approved): Owner approved a freeze on dependency upgrades. NEWER (owner approved): Owner lifted the dependency upgrade freeze. Answer: {"replaces": true, "thing": "the dependency upgrade freeze"}

Answer with one JSON object in exactly that shape and nothing else.
